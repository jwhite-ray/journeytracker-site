Recommended moves, in order

1  High conviction  $0 · lineup move

MOVE Tank Dell (WR, HOU) to the IR slot  ·  Do it first, since it opens the roster spot for move 3

The league settings pulled today list one IR slot in the lineup configuration. That contradicts last week's report, which found none, so it was either added since or misread then. Check in the ESPN app before relying on it: Dell should show an option to move to IR. He is on injured reserve with a return designation, cannot play until Week 5 at the earliest (October 11 vs. Tennessee), and is coming back from a multi-ligament knee reconstruction.

If the slot is real, this costs nothing: Dell stays as a free stash, and Justin's 14th roster spot opens without a drop. If the slot isn't there, Dell becomes the drop in move 3, for the same reasons as last week. Nobody else has used IR either; Team Stowell has A.J. Brown sitting on the bench with the same designation.

2  Medium-high conviction  Waiver claim · $2

ADD Kaelon Black (RB, SF)  ·  DROP Kaleb Johnson (RB, GB)  ·  Bid $2, placed before about 1:00am MT Wednesday

The drop first, because the $11 is gone either way. Johnson dressed, was healthy, and got zero offensive snaps. Green Bay's backfield was Chris Brooks on third downs and in the two-minute drill (80% of his snaps) and MarShawn Lloyd on early downs (13 carries). Josh Jacobs pleaded no contest on September 10 and remains on the Commissioner's Exempt List. Analysts expect a suspension near the six-game guideline, but that doesn't help Johnson: the replacement roles have already gone to two other backs. At best he's the fourth back on a roster with a two-man split. His national rostered share has fallen to 5.9%.

Why Black. McCaffrey is not hurt; he played into the fourth quarter and led the 49ers with 8 targets. The Week 1 split looks planned. Black played 44% of snaps, got 14 carries to McCaffrey's 10, took 45% of the running-back opportunities, and was on the field in every quarter. Beat writers expect McCaffrey's workload to settle below his usual share after a summer of missed practice time. He's 30 and coming off an NFL-high 413 touches. That makes Black two things at once:

  • a real RB3 today, especially in expected blowouts (San Francisco is a 10.5-point favorite against Miami this week)
  • an immediate RB1 if McCaffrey misses time

Right now Justin's RB3 is Dobbins, with 8 carries and no targets on 40% of snaps. Black beats that before any injury.

The bid. Black is the top add nationally, listed by 9 of 12 waiver columns reviewed, with expert FAAB ranging from $2 to $20 (typically $5–9). None of that predicts this league. The only manager with a clear reason to want him is Team Stowell, who rosters McCaffrey. Stowell is also the most active manager here, but has made every move as a $0 free-agent add and has never placed a waiver claim. So there's probably no competition, and $1 probably wins. Because Justin loses ties, though, $1 is the one bid that can lose to a token claim. $2 covers that for one extra dollar. Going higher pays for competition this league has never shown.

Bye check: Black's bye is Week 8, the same as Kittle's. With four running backs rostered it's fine, but it adds to how thin that week already is.

3  Medium conviction  $0 · wait for free agency

ADD Pat Freiermuth (TE, PIT)  ·  DROP nobody if Dell is on IR; otherwise Tank Dell  ·  Add him Wednesday morning after waivers clear

Last week's top recommendation, a second tight end, wasn't made. Week 1 made the case stronger. Kittle is on a snap count: 46% of snaps and 36% of routes, with every target on the opening possession. San Francisco's backup tight end, Jake Tonges, hurt his MCL, which could eventually push Kittle's workload back up, but not this week. And the Week 8 bye is still a guaranteed zero in a required slot.

Freiermuth is the best fit in the pool, for reasons that show up in the usage data rather than the box score. He ran a 72% route share, a big jump from last season. Darnell Washington ran only 35%. And Pittsburgh passed more than any team relative to expectation in Week 1. His bye is Week 9, so he actually covers Week 8. Against Miami's likely blowout script, Freiermuth at New England could be a legitimate start over a snap-limited Kittle this week. Thursday's lineup check will make that call.

The tight ends skipped, and why:

  • Mike Gesicki (5-78-1): only 33% of snaps and 17 routes. A one-week spike.
  • Noah Fant: 28% route share. Also a spike, and his bye is Week 8.
  • Dalton Schultz: good role, but a Week 8 bye makes him useless for Kittle's.
  • Brenton Strange: last week's pick, but only 3 targets. Secure snaps, thin volume.
  • Hunter Henry (bye 11) and Evan Engram (bye 10): fine fallbacks if Freiermuth is gone.
  • T.J. Hockenson (bye 6): targets are capped with Carson Wentz likely starting.

Why no bid: Freiermuth is 14.6% rostered nationally, Justin doesn't need him before Sunday, and there are three equivalent fallbacks. Of the two single-tight-end teams, Lackey and Carnahan, neither has ever placed a claim. This is the league's default case: let him clear and add him for $0.

Let them have one. The name for Justin to revisit in Week 5–6 is still whoever is running best; Young (bye 5) and Shough (bye 8) are both clear of Week 7.

How confident to be in all this

More than last week, since there's one real game of snap and route data. It's still one game, though, and several signals are distorted:

  • Black's 14 carries came in a lopsided game, and one national outlet mentioned McCaffrey cramping.
  • Freiermuth's 72% route share is a single data point for a player who has never held that role for a full season.
  • Johnson's zero snaps came one week after a trade. A back who learned the playbook in ten days could get more work. The problem is who's ahead of him, not how much he knows.

The IR slot is the item most likely to be wrong. The API shows it, last week's report said otherwise, and it's worth a ten-second check in the app before planning around it.

The FAAB read is now four data points (three bids plus one cancelled claim) and still points one way. But Bijan Mustard's cancelled claim is new, and a manager who needs a quarterback is the kind who bids. It doesn't affect Justin's targets this week.
