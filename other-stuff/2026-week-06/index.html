What the table says

Team Jensen lost for the first time and still scored a point. Jensen put up 136.76, the third-best score of the week, and ran into Team Carnahan's season-high 164.16. That is the format working as designed: Jensen is now five for five on top-five finishes, which is why they lead by two points despite Shough Stopper having 35 more points for.

Shough Stopper and Team Carvalho show the other side. Both are 4–1 with the league's best and fourth-best records, but Shough has three top-five weeks and Carvalho only two. Carvalho won Week 5 with 128.04 and missed the top five by 2.64 points. They are tied at 6 with His Airdness and Team Lackey, who are each only 3–2.

Team Carnahan is 1–4 and has three top-five weeks. That is 4 points, the same as Team Whitehead at 2–3. Cee Deez Nuts (2–3) sits a point ahead of both on the strength of three top-five finishes. In this league, losing to the week's top scorer only costs half the week.

The cut line keeps landing near 130 and keeps being close. Three of the five weeks were decided by less than four points at the top-five line. The cut has climbed from about 110 to 131 as rosters settle. A team that wants to stay in the top-five race needs a lineup that can reach the low 130s, which rewards starting the higher-ceiling FLEX option over the safer one.

Who is going to bid on Woody Marks

Marks is this week's consensus #1 running back add (NFL.com, Bleacher Report, RotoBaller). Bleacher Report suggests $15–25 in a $100 league. In this league, the last three consensus top-RB adds went for $51 (Braelon Allen), $17 (Ollie Gordon) and $8 (Shipley, who was obviously temporary). Here are the teams with both a need and a bidding history:

  • Team Carvalho: the most likely winner. They own Montgomery, so Marks is their handcuff. They also paid $51 to cover an injured starter in Week 4. But they already roster eight running backs, and they have only $33 left. Their maximum possible bid is $33.
  • His Airdness: a real threat. They have $85 and have bid $30 and $15. Their RB2 is Javonte Williams. They win ties against Justin.
  • Team Stowell and Team Lackey: they need RBs but don't bid. Stowell's largest bid ever is $6. Lackey has never placed a winning claim.
  • Everyone else has a full backfield or little FAAB.

The expected winning price is in the high teens to mid-20s, unless Carvalho goes all in. $27 beats every bid His Airdness has placed except the $30 on Allen, and still leaves Justin $55. The only way to be sure of winning is $34, one dollar more than Carvalho's whole budget. That is 41% of Justin's FAAB for about two weeks as a starter followed by a backup role, and I would not pay it.

The moves, in order

1. Add Woody Marks (RB, Texans): bid $27, drop Keaton Mitchell

Conviction: medium-high on the player, medium on winning at this price · Expected competition: Carvalho (likely), His Airdness (possible) · $1 wins? No

Before the injury, Marks was already scoring 8 to 12 points a week as Houston's change-of-pace back. When Montgomery left Week 5 early, Marks took 19 touches for 85 yards, and Ryans said he "will carry the load." Montgomery is week-to-week, so assume Marks leads the backfield for Week 6 (London, against Jacksonville, 7:30am MT Sunday) and probably Week 7. Houston's bye is Week 8. After Montgomery returns, Marks is still a 9-point committee back. That is more than Mitchell offers now.

Why it fits Justin's roster: the bye map leaves an RB-sized hole in the FLEX for each of the next two weeks. Mitchell and White both have Week 7 byes, and Mitchell is now third in snaps in his own backfield. Replacing about 6 projected points with 12 to 14 in a starting slot is worth 12 to 16 points over two weeks. Three of five cut lines this season were decided by under four points. Marks also takes the obvious handcuff from Carvalho, Justin's opponent this week.

If he loses: keep Mitchell, and start Dobbins and White in the RB/FLEX spots. Check Wednesday morning for Kendre Miller or Dameon Pierce as free adds.

2. Pass on Germie Bernard, Kendrick Bourne and the TE wave

Conviction: high

Germie Bernard (Steelers, now the WR2 with Pittman on IR) is the best receiver on the wire. He would not start for Justin over Flowers, McLaurin, Williams, Odunze or Washington. Strange, Freiermuth, Henry and Higbee are all good streaming tight ends. Justin has Kittle, and Andrews covers the bye. Those bids can go to teams that need those positions.

3. Keep Rachaad White for one more week

Conviction: medium

White's results look like a drop: 3.0 points, and 0.0 the week before because of injury. His usage says otherwise. He played 71% of snaps, all of the third-down snaps, and 81% of early-down snaps in 11 personnel. Austin Ekeler played only six snaps. Snap share predicts future points better than one bad game does. White becomes the next drop if Marks is won and a Wednesday free agent (Pierce, if Barkley is out) is worth more.

Wait and get them free on Wednesday

Most of the pool will still be there Wednesday morning. Last week, only five claims cleared on the Wednesday run, and the rest of the pool went for $0. As always, grab anything you want before Bijan Mustard's early-morning adds.

  • Dameon Pierce (RB, Eagles, 2% rostered): only matters if Barkley misses another week. Shipley (plantar fascia) and Bigsby (IR) are both out, so Pierce would lead Philadelphia's backfield against Carolina. Watch Wednesday's practice report. If Barkley does not practice, drop White for Pierce.
  • Kendre Miller (RB, Saints): Bleacher Report suggests $4–8. Kamara's back injury makes him the most likely Saints back to see work. Justin has no need for him unless Barkley's injury gets worse.
  • Kimani Vidal (RB, Chargers): he is the reason Mitchell is the drop. Don't swap one Chargers committee back for another. Both have Week 7 byes.
  • Week 7 QB streamers: Jameis Winston, Kirk Cousins, Sam Darnold and Bo Nix are all unrostered. Next Tuesday's report will pick one based on matchups.

Drops, justified

  • Keaton Mitchell first. His snap share fell from first to second among Chargers backs in one week, and the team calls it a committee. He has a Week 7 bye, and his ESPN rest-of-season projection (97) ties White's for the lowest among Justin's running backs, while White's usage is better. Most importantly, he will not play in Week 7, which is the week Marks would be most useful.
  • Not Concepcion. He is in the concussion protocol, but he ran 100% of routes before the injury and his bye has passed. One missed week is not a reason to drop him.
  • Not Andrews. He is the Week 8 TE and has no other use until then. A free-agent TE could replace him that week, but he costs nothing to keep for two more weeks.

How confident to be in all this

The Marks call is solid as a player evaluation. Montgomery's timeline is the main uncertainty. "Week-to-week" could mean only one game, and if Montgomery returns in Week 7 Marks is a one-week starter before his bye. The bid amount is a guess about two specific managers. Carvalho may bid all $33 to protect Montgomery's backfield, and if so, Justin should let him. $27 is a price for a player who helps Justin win a standings point or two, not a price for winning at any cost.
