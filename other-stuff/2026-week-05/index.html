The moves, in order

1. Add Will Shipley (RB, Eagles): bid $19, drop Brian Thomas Jr.

Conviction: medium-high on the player, medium on the price. 0.8% rostered on ESPN · FantasyPros' #1 priority add this week ($12 true value, $25 if desperate) · CBS suggests 10% · PHI bye Week 10 · Week 5 vs. JAX (London), Week 6 and beyond depend on Barkley

Other managers see Shipley as a one- or two-week fill-in. For Justin he has three uses. This week, he is the lead back on an Eagles offense whose running back has been a top-tier fantasy producer. Justin's alternatives at RB2 and FLEX are a Chargers committee back and a Denver back losing his role. In Week 7, with Mitchell and White on bye, he may be needed even if Barkley is back. For the rest of the season, he is the handcuff for a 29-year-old with a hamstring injury, on the team whose running back matters most in fantasy. When Barkley returns, Shipley's value drops to near zero for everyone except Barkley's owner.

Why $19: it beats every running-back bid in league history except the Braelon Allen bidding war. It is $2 above Bijan's Gordon bid, and Justin wins ties against Bijan and Stowell anyway. It does not chase His Airdness to $30, the price of a long-term starter. If His Airdness pays $30 or more, let Shipley go. Justin would keep $83 for a later week. The fallback is free on Wednesday: Austin Ekeler or Tyler Allgeier for this week, and Dameon Pierce if Philadelphia promotes him.

The risk: Philadelphia could split the work with a practice-squad back or trade for a veteran. Shipley has never been a starter. He also loses most of his value the week Barkley returns. $19 is a premium for insurance, and Justin is the one manager who gets the full benefit of that insurance.

The drop: Thomas has been benched for two weeks running and is Jacksonville's third receiver in their two-tight-end sets. He is also one of the five Week 7 byes. Justin's other receivers (Flowers, Williams, McLaurin, Odunze and Concepcion) are all playing more than he is right now. When Tyson returns around Week 9, one more receiver will have to go anyway.

2. Pass on Tyreek Hill (WR, free agent)

Conviction: medium. 29.2% rostered · dropped by Team Stowell on October 5 · CBS's headline add · Drew Rosenhaus expects him to sign "this week," with Kansas City the likely landing spot

If he signs with Kansas City, Hill has a real ceiling. The Chiefs have lost Rashee Rice for now and Tyquan Thornton for the season. But he is 32, coming back from a 2025 knee injury, has no team yet, and Kansas City is on bye this week. The earliest he could help is Week 6, and his role would be uncertain. Justin's receivers are not the problem, and paying FAAB for a sixth receiver means outbidding the national hype. If he goes unclaimed, adding him for free on Wednesday in place of Concepcion would be reasonable. Bidding is not worth it.

3. Pass on Keon Coleman (WR, Bills)

Conviction: medium. 4.3% rostered · 6 catches for 116 yards and a TD with DJ Moore out

DJ Moore's shoulder (AC joint) is not a long-term injury, and Buffalo plays the Rams on Monday night. Coleman is a one-week start with a short-term role, and Justin has five starting-caliber receivers. Bijan Mustard is the most likely bidder.

4. Keep J.K. Dobbins, but don't count on him

Conviction: medium. 21 snaps to Harvey's 29 in Week 4

Dobbins is losing playing time, and Coleman's return in Week 7 will cut into it further. He still gets the early-down carries in a decent offense, though. With Barkley hurt and two other backs on bye in Week 7, Justin needs every running back who has a role. He becomes a drop when Barkley is healthy again.
