What the table says

The round robin is over, so the race resets to scoring alone. Eight weeks remain at 2 points per top-five finish, which is 16 points still available to every team. Team Jensen (15) leads Shough Stopper (13) by two, and Team Carvalho has 11. A three-way tie at 10 (Lackey, His Airdness, Stowell) is separated only by points for. Every team except This Sucks is within 9 points of first. That's less than five top-five weeks.

Shough Stopper is the team to beat from here. It leads the league in points for by 123 (1,243.52 to Jensen's 1,120.40) and has made the top five in seven of nine weeks. From now on, scoring is the only thing that counts, and Shough has scored the most. Team Carvalho went 7–2 but made the top five only four times. Seven of its 11 points came from matchup wins that are no longer available, so expect it to slide unless its scoring picks up.

The cut line to beat is about 112. Across nine weeks the 5th-place score ran from 106.28 to 130.68, with a median of 112.48. Four of the nine weeks were decided by less than three points, and three by under 0.4. Week 9's cut (106.28) was the lowest of the season. A team that scores 115 every week makes the top five most weeks, and that is now worth 2 points rather than 1. Ceiling still matters, but with no matchup to win, avoiding an 80-point dud matters as much as a 150-point spike.

Week 9 was the floor for this table. Five teams finished under 104, and This Sucks is still at zero points after nine weeks. The middle is tight. Places 4 through 9 are separated by 4 points, which is two top-five weeks.

The ranked moves

0. Required: Tyson from IR to the bench — drop Hunter Henry

Conviction: high · no FAAB · do it before claims process at 1:00am MT Wednesday

Tyson was activated off injured reserve and played Sunday. As long as an ACTIVE player sits in the IR slot, ESPN treats the roster as illegal and rejects adds and claims. Moving him to the bench makes 15 active players, so one has to go.

Why Henry. He was added for Kittle's Week 8 bye, and Kittle has no bye left. Henry's last two games were 6.0 and 6.5, he's on bye in Week 11, and the foot injury has lingered. If Kittle gets hurt, the TE wire has Darren Waller (23.0 in Week 9), AJ Barner and Mike Gesicki available for $0. Malik Washington is the other candidate, but Waddle and Odunze are both out this week, so the WR depth is worth more to you this week than a backup TE.

1. Add Kaleb Johnson (RB, GB) — bid $7 — drop Keaton Mitchell

Conviction: medium on the add, low–medium on him mattering past Week 10 · 1% rostered on ESPN · bye Week 11

The role. Jacobs fractured his right ring finger early in Week 9 and is having surgery this week. CBS's Matt Zenitz puts the absence at three weeks at most, and LaFleur said IR is "a possibility." Lloyd (knee) was inactive Sunday and might return for Week 10. Johnson, a healthy scratch the two weeks before, took over and had the Packers' best rushing day of the season, 14-68-1. The likely shape is a lead-back share vs. Minnesota (not an easy run defense), then a bye, then maybe Week 12 if Jacobs isn't back. He's a former Whitehead pickup, $11 in week zero.

Who else bids. Nationally this is a mid-list add (#7 in ClutchPoints/Yahoo's top 10). The consensus #1 and #3 (Audric Estime and Seth McGowan) are already rostered here by Lackey and Carvalho, which makes Corey Kiner this league's top available name. Expect the usual money (His Airdness's habitual $15, and Jensen, who owns Stevenson) to go to Kiner, not Johnson.

  • Team Stowell ($98, waiver #3) owns Jacobs, so this is a handcuff decision for it. Its biggest bid all season is $6, and it usually takes $0 free agents the morning after. $7 beats every bid it has made.
  • Team Lackey ($100, #2) has never placed a bid. It has lost Monangai and TreVeyon Henderson, but it just added Estime and has Dowdle and Dobbins.
  • His Airdness ($15) and Team Jensen ($75) are more likely to bid on Kiner.

Why only $7. Johnson replaces your second FLEX (Malik Washington or Tyson, projected about 9) for one week. That's worth a couple of points, and maybe a few more in Week 12. In Week 9 the cut line was beaten by less than 4 points, so it isn't nothing. But $15–20, the price of the consensus #1 add here, would be paying a starter's price for a one-week fill-in. If he's outbid, you lose little.

Why Mitchell is the drop. He's been the drop candidate for five straight weeks. He's third in a committee and has nothing to offer in Weeks 10–17 that Johnson or a free-agent back can't match.

2. Backup claim: Corey Kiner (RB, NE) — bid $3 — drop Keaton Mitchell (rank it below Johnson)

Conviction: low · 6% rostered · bye Week 11

Stevenson (plantaris tendon in the knee) is expected to miss the Lions game in Munich, and TreVeyon Henderson has been out with a neurological illness that has no timeline. That leaves Kiner as the Week 10 lead back, with Lan Larison behind him. He ran 8-55-1 in Week 9 and is averaging 4.9 yards per carry. Most columns list him as a top-five add, with suggested FAAB of $10–20. The matchup is bad (Detroit), it's a 7:30am MT kickoff, New England is on bye in Week 11, and Stevenson could be back in Week 12.

Why $3. This is the league's consensus #1 available add, so expect the usual $15. Jensen owns Stevenson, has $75, beats you on ties, and has bid $15 and $9 before. His Airdness has exactly $15 and has bid exactly $15 four times. You won't win a bidding war here, and shouldn't try. The $3 is a lottery ticket in case everyone else spends elsewhere. Because it uses the same drop as the Johnson claim, it can't also go through if Johnson wins.

3. Hold Waddle and Flowers

Conviction: medium · no move

Waddle's concussion falls on Denver's bye, so he should be back on Nov. 22, and dropping him now would mean selling at his lowest value. Flowers gets a veteran QB back (Brissett, acquired at the deadline) after two games with almost no production. Both drops are worse than Mitchell or Henry, and you only need to make two.
