What the table says

The two-point system rewards a very different team than a straight win-loss record does, and Team Whitehead is currently on the right side of that. Justin is 1–1 but tied for second in points, because he has the only pair of top-five scoring weeks in the league outside of Team Jensen. A 1–1 record is 9th-best; 3 points is 4th.

Team Stowell is the mirror image and the cautionary tale: also 1–1, but zero top-five weeks, so they sit on a single point, tied with two 0–2 teams. Consistent mid-range scoring is worth almost nothing here. His Airdness has the second-most points for in the league (248.78) and only 2 points, because 150.12 and 98.66 averages out fine and cashes in only once.

The cut line is the number to watch, and it moved a lot. Week 1 required 118.06 to make the top five. Week 2 required 109.02 — and sixth place missed by 0.32 points. That margin is the practical argument for treating every FLEX decision as live: in this format, three points of lineup optimization is a real chance at a full standings point, which a head-to-head blowout win never gives you.

Scoring format note: through Week 9, every team plays every other team once and both points are live (matchup win + top five, maximum two per week). The schedule confirms the round robin completes exactly at Week 9. From Week 10 the matchups stop counting and the weekly top five earns two points each.

The moves, in order

1. Add Tank Bigsby (RB, Philadelphia) — bid $3 — drop Caleb Douglas

Conviction: medium-high. 15.2% rostered · Week 2: 15 touches, 38 yards, 1 TD

Barkley left on the first play at Tennessee with a stinger and finished with 12 snaps. He is expected to play Monday night, and the MRI was called precautionary — this is insurance, not a reaction. But Barkley is Justin's RB2 on a three-back roster, and the replacement level behind him is Rome Odunze in a FLEX spot.

The part the national columns are glossing over: in Barkley's absence, Will Shipley played more snaps than Bigsby — 39 (51%) to 26 (34%) — because Philadelphia trusts him in pass protection. Bigsby got the touches (15 to 9) and the touchdown; Shipley got the passing downs. This is a genuine committee, not a clean handcuff, and in PPR that caps Bigsby's ceiling meaningfully. He is still the right half of the split to own, because the goal-line and early-down work is where the points are in that offense, but it is a reason to bid $3 and not $8.

Expected competition: very low. Nobody else in the league owns Barkley, so nobody else has a specific reason to want Bigsby. Bijan Mustard might throw $1 at him out of habit, and Justin beats him on the tie. $2 probably wins this. $3 is the insurance premium.

The drop: Caleb Douglas is the clearest cut on the roster. He is the 38%-rostered third receiver in a bad Miami offense, and his Week 1 line (14.4) has looked like the outlier ever since. Justin has eight receivers for two WR slots and two FLEX spots; Douglas is the eighth.

2. Add a quarterback for Week 7 — bid $2 on Jared Goff — drop Jayden Reed

Conviction on the need: certain. Conviction on the urgency: low. Goff is 69.3% rostered nationally · Week 2: 29.8 points · DET bye is Week 6, so he is available in Week 7

Week 7 is four weeks out and Justin currently cannot field a quarterback that week. The cheapest possible fix is to take one now, while a genuinely startable option is sitting free: Jared Goff at 69% national rostership, Bo Nix at 83%, C.J. Stroud, Tyler Shough, Kyler Murray and Geno Smith are all unrostered in this league.

Honest framing: this is a $2 convenience purchase, not a necessity. A 10-team league with one QB slot and no kickers or defenses will never run out of quarterbacks. Bijan Mustard alone has cycled Kyler Murray, Bryce Young, Baker Mayfield and Tyler Shough through his roster in three weeks. If this claim loses, Justin picks up a comparable quarterback in Week 6 for $0 and nothing is lost.

Expected competition: real, but not for Goff specifically. Team Carvalho has zero healthy quarterbacks, $95, and a demonstrated willingness to bid. They will take the quarterback they rate highest, and Carvalho beats Justin on a tie. Do not chase — if they want Goff at $12, let them have him. There are six more.

The drop: Jayden Reed was carted off against the Jets and re-diagnosed with a back injury, timeline TBD, and is not expected to be cleared for Thursday. He is the sixth-best healthy receiver on a roster that starts two. The honest counter-argument: Reed at 64.6% rostered has more upside than Douglas if the back injury is short, and dropping two receivers in one week to add two bench pieces is aggressive. If only one claim is made, make it Bigsby and keep Reed.

3. Pass on Jonah Coleman (RB, Denver) — the consensus national #1 add

Conviction: medium. 22.2% rostered and climbing · Week 2: 10-39-1 rushing, 3-19 receiving, 14.8 points

Every major outlet has Coleman at or near the top of the Week 3 board, and the logic is clean: Dobbins and RJ Harvey both went down with hamstrings, and Coleman led the backfield with 25 snaps. Three reasons to let him go anyway.

It is a four-man committee, not a vacated role. Coleman 25 snaps, Dobbins 21, Tyler Badie 16. Even as the "lead back," Coleman got 10 carries for 39 yards — the 14.8 was a touchdown, not a workload. Denver's backfield has been unproductive for fantasy all season, and adding a fourth claimant to it does not change that.

Dobbins is expected back Sunday night. The hamstring is reported as not serious. Justin already owns Dobbins. Spending FAAB to own a second piece of a bad committee that Justin is already exposed to is the wrong direction — it doubles down on the weakest asset class on the roster rather than diversifying away from it.

Team Jensen has the specific need and the bid history. They own RJ Harvey, they have $91, they paid $9 for Devaughn Vele, and they beat Justin on a tie. This is the one player this week where the "size up for a specific manager with a demonstrated need" rule applies — and the honest conclusion is that the player is not worth what it would cost to win him. If Justin wants him anyway, the number is $10–12, not $3. I would not spend it.

Drops, justified

Opportunity cost, not "worst player." Justin's roster is full, so the question for each drop is what that bench spot is worth relative to what it could hold.

  • Caleb Douglas (drop first). Eighth receiver on a roster that starts two plus two FLEX. He is Miami's WR3 in a weak offense, questionable, and his 38.7% rostership is the lowest of any healthy player Justin owns. The spot is worth more as Barkley insurance than as a fourth dart throw at the same position.
  • Jayden Reed (drop second, only if making both claims). A back injury serious enough to require a cart, with no timeline, on a receiver who was third in Green Bay's target order when healthy. The counter-argument is real — 64.6% rostered is not nothing, and if the injury is two weeks he is better than what replaces him. This is the closest call on the page.
  • Do not drop KC Concepcion. 72% route share and Cleveland's second target behind Denzel Boston, scoring 7.8 and 8.2. He is a better asset than either of the above.
  • Do not drop Mark Andrews. He was added on Sep 18 specifically because his Week 13 bye does not collide with Kittle's Week 8 bye. That is the whole point of carrying him.
  • Do not drop Jordyn Tyson. He is on IR and costs no active roster spot. Eligible Week 5 at the earliest, realistically mid-October, and he was the 8th overall pick in an offense whose quarterback is averaging 23 points a game.

How confident to be in all this

  • The FAAB read is the strongest part. Eight bids, two waiver runs, five teams at $100 with zero claims. The pattern is consistent and the sample is now large enough to act on. The one correction: prices are drifting up, and last week's $2 recommendation was too low.
  • The Bigsby recommendation is a bet on a committee. Shipley out-snapped him. If Philadelphia leans pass-protection-first with Barkley healthy, Bigsby is a bench player who never starts. $3 is sized for that.
  • The Coleman pass is the call most likely to look wrong. If Dobbins' hamstring lingers two or three weeks and Harvey stays out, Coleman becomes a weekly starter and Jensen gets him for $10. That is a real outcome and the reasoning above accepts it. The judgment is that a four-way Denver committee is not worth $12 of a $85 budget in Week 3.
  • Barkley and Dobbins are both reported as likely to play. Both reports are Monday-and-Tuesday optimism, and both players carry Sunday/Monday game slots, so the real answer arrives after claims have already processed. That asymmetry is the argument for the $3 on Bigsby rather than waiting.
  • The Week 7 quarterback hole is the only certainty on this page. Everything else is probabilistic. That one is on the schedule.
